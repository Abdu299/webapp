POSTER TEXT: PRIVACY NETWORK (Victoria)
All numbers come from the files in output/ (made by src/privacy_network/visualization.ipynb).
"Hidden" = the model's top guess is more than 750 km from where the photo was taken
(GeoViT: it names the wrong country). It is always counted on the photos that model
locates without blur.


TITLE (pick one)
----------------
Question: Can one forward pass hide where a photo was taken?
Finding:  One forward pass hides photos from a geolocator 14,000x faster than per-photo optimization


MOTIVATION (two lines)
----------------------
Platforms strip GPS metadata, but the pixels still give the location away: the OSV-5M model
places 61% (145 of 237) of everyday Im2GPS photos within 750 km.
We look for the smallest blur that stops this, fast enough to run when a photo is uploaded.


CONTRIBUTIONS (the rubric grades these, so list them)
-----------------------------------------------------
1. A privacy network that blurs a photo in one forward pass so a geolocation model can no
   longer place it within 750 km. It adapts Dabkowski & Gal's real-time saliency network to
   privacy and is trained without labels: a frozen geolocator is the teacher.
2. A comparison with full blur, heatmap blur (where William's occlusion heatmaps are hottest)
   and per-photo optimized blur (Fong et al.) on privacy, image quality (LPIPS, SSIM), speed,
   and whether the protection survives JPEG compression and screenshots, on everyday photos
   (Im2GPS) and street photos (OSV-5M).
3. The same network trained against our own geolocator (GeoViT), and a transfer test of every
   blur on models it was not made for, including GeoCLIP, which no method was trained against.


RELATED WORK AND HOW WE DIFFER (shared section, but these are the works behind your part)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- PlaNet [1] and OSV-5M [12]: geolocation as classification into cells of the Earth. We use the
  published OSV-5M model as the model to fool. PlaNet's occlusion analysis is the idea behind
  the heatmaps used by our heatmap blur baseline.
- Fong et al. [6]: optimizes, for each image, the smallest smooth mask that changes a
  classifier's answer, to explain it. We use it as a privacy baseline (the target becomes
  "move the guess beyond 750 km"). It takes 252 s per photo.
- Dabkowski & Gal [7]: train a network that predicts such a mask in one pass, in real time, to
  explain a classifier. We adapt it to privacy: only the "destroy" goal is kept (lower the
  probability of every cell within 750 km of the truth), the mask sets a blur strength per
  pixel, and the frozen geolocator's own ViT is the encoder.
- GeoCLIP [16]: a geolocator that matches photos to GPS coordinates. Used only to test transfer.
- SSIM [14] and LPIPS [15]: how much the photo changed.
New reference: [16] V. Vivanco Cepeda, G. K. Nayak, M. Shah, "GeoCLIP: Clip-inspired alignment
between locations and images for effective worldwide geo-localization", NeurIPS 2023.


METHOD (one diagram: poster_method.png)
---------------------------------------
- The privacy network: the frozen ViT encoder of the geolocator (16x16 patch tokens) and a
  small convolutional decoder (trained) that outputs a blur mask m, one value 0-1 per pixel.
- Each pixel is blurred by its value in m, using Fong et al.'s blur pyramid (up to sigma 20).
- Training only: the frozen geolocator guesses on the blurred photo, and the loss is
      loss = log P_near + lambda_area * mean(m) + lambda_tv * smoothness(m)
  P_near = the probability the geolocator gives the cells within 750 km of the true location.
  The first term hides the photo, the second pays for every blurred pixel, the third prefers
  smooth masks. Photos that are already hidden get a floor on log P_near (0.01), so they are
  not blurred further for no gain.
- No labels are needed: the frozen geolocator is the teacher. Only the decoder is trained.
- lambda_area sets the trade-off between privacy and image damage (3 = main setting).
- Protecting a photo is one forward pass: 0.018 s with the OSV-5M encoder, 0.008 s with GeoViT.


SETUP (small table)
-------------------
Data          Train / validation: 42,236 / 4,596 OSV-5M street photos from its test split (unseen
              by the OSV-5M model), split by sequence. Validation was used to choose lambda_area.
              Test: Im2GPS, 237 everyday photos, and 1,233 OSV-5M street photos.
Models        OSV-5M baseline (CLIP ViT-L/14, frozen): trained against, main model.
              GeoViT (Abdulrahman, DINOv2-small, 214 countries, frozen): trained against, second model.
              GeoCLIP: only for transfer.
Located       Im2GPS: OSV-5M 145, GeoViT 54, GeoCLIP 182 of 237. Street test: 812, 770, 531 of 1,233.
Metrics       Hidden (%), blurred area (mean mask), LPIPS (lower = less damage), SSIM (higher =
              more similar), seconds per photo (RTX 5070).
Baselines     Full blur (Gaussian sigma 20); heatmap blur (hottest 10% of the occlusion heatmap
              fully blurred, the rest in proportion to heat); optimized blur (Fong et al., 1,600
              steps, 5-80% area targets).
Training      5 epochs, AdamW (lr 3e-4), batch 16, lambda_area 3, lambda_tv 1.
              69 min (OSV-5M) and 33 min (GeoViT) on an RTX 5070. 3 seeds for the main network.

Experiments and their goals:
E1  Against OSV-5M: how many photos does each method hide, how much does it damage the photo,
    and how long does it take? (poster_table_osv5m.png, poster_tradeoff_osv5m.png)
E2  Against our own GeoViT: does the same method work for a different geolocator?
    (poster_tradeoff_geovit.png, poster_table_geovit.png)
E3  Transfer: does a blur made against one model also fool models it was not made for,
    including GeoCLIP, which no method saw? (poster_plot_transfer.png, poster_table_transfer.png)
E4  Where and why it fails: example photos, overlap with the heatmaps, seeds, street photos

E5  Robustness: is the photo still hidden after it is saved as a JPEG (quality 75) or

RESULTS (main figure: poster_tradeoff_osv5m.png; the same numbers in poster_table_osv5m.png)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Against OSV-5M on Im2GPS (145 photos):
  Full blur                 100% blurred   95% hidden   LPIPS 0.82   0.0001 s
  Heatmap blur              48%            76%          0.56         7.5 s
  Optimized blur, 5%        11%            94%          0.25         252 s
  Optimized blur, 10%       16%            98%          0.33         252 s
  Privacy network, lambda 3 16 +- 3%       92 +- 6%     0.39 +- 0.03 0.018 s   (3 seeds)
  Privacy network, lambda 10 7%            81%          0.18         0.018 s
Against GeoViT on Im2GPS (54 photos):
  Full blur 81% hidden; heatmap blur (GeoViT's heatmap, 39% blurred) 76%, 0.72 s;
  privacy network 96% with 7% blurred, LPIPS 0.18, 0.008 s.

Takeaway (one sentence):
One forward pass hides 92% of photos from OSV-5M in 0.02 s, about 14,000x faster than
per-photo optimization (252 s), which still damages the photo less for the same privacy.


ANALYSIS (failure cases and what drives the difference)
-------------------------------------------------------
Failure cases (poster_examples.png):
- Full blur still finds the Sydney Opera House (399 km): its shape and colours survive heavy blur.
  Full blur also hides only 81% from GeoViT, while destroying the photo (LPIPS 0.82).
- Heatmap blur leaves Sydney at 2 km: blurring where the model looks is not enough.
- The network trained against GeoViT barely blurs Sydney, and OSV-5M finds it (21 km).
What drives the differences:
- Per-photo search against one pass: optimized blur searches each photo for 1,600 steps, so it
  fits that photo better (94% hidden at LPIPS 0.25, against 92% at 0.39 for the network), but it
  takes 252 s. The network learned one general rule from 42k photos, so it runs in 0.018 s.
  This is the trade Dabkowski & Gal made for real-time saliency, now measured for privacy.
- Heatmaps do not say what to blur: heatmap blur needs 39-48% of the photo and still hides only
  76%, from both models. The network's blur overlaps the heatmaps only 1.2x more than chance
  (GeoViT: 1.17x). Occlusion heatmaps (as in PlaNet) cover one window at a time, while the
  models seem to use several cues at once.
- Blurs specialise to their model (poster_table_transfer.png): each blur works best on the model
  it was made against. Among the blurs made against OSV-5M, the network blurs the least (13%)
  yet hides the most from GeoViT (80%) and GeoCLIP (62%); optimized blur gets 56% and 35%,
  heatmap blur 65% and 54% with 48% blurred. The GeoViT network's light 7% blur hides only 57%
  from OSV-5M and 27% from GeoCLIP.
- The masks are textures and stripes, not objects, and two seeds overlap only 1.3x more than
  chance: many different blurs work, which looks like model-specific (adversarial) patterns.
- Compression and screenshots (poster_table_robustness.png): the two targeted blurs lose
  protection. Against OSV-5M, optimized blur drops from 97% to 81% (JPEG) and 80% (screenshot),
  the network from 92% to 84% and 83%. The broad blurs do not drop (full blur 94-96%, heatmap
  blur 74-79%). After compression the network is about as private as optimized blur, with far
  less blur than heatmap blur. The GeoViT network's light 7% blur drops most on a screenshot
  (96% to 72%). This fits fine, model-specific patterns that compression and resizing smooth away.
- Domain: on OSV-5M street photos, like the training photos, the network hides 98 +- 2% at
  LPIPS 0.21 (3 seeds; full blur 97%). On everyday Im2GPS photos it hides less (92%) and blurs more.
- Too high an area cost collapses the network to no blur (lambda_area 30 hides 1% on validation).


LIMITATIONS AND NEXT STEPS
--------------------------
Limitations:
- The protection is tuned to the model it was trained against; other models are fooled less
  (e.g. 52% for GeoViT on street photos), so an unknown attacker may not be fooled.
- Small samples: 54 Im2GPS photos for GeoViT (about +-11 points), 145 for OSV-5M (about +-4).
  Saving as PNG adds about 1 point of noise.
- Only the main network has 3 seeds; other settings and all transfer numbers are one seed.
- Optimized blur was only run against OSV-5M, and the baselines only on Im2GPS (full blur on both).
- Compression tested at one setting only (JPEG quality 75), and a screenshot is simulated as a
  half-size copy, not a real screen capture.
- 224x224 centre crop only. LPIPS and SSIM stand in for what people see; no user study.
Next steps:
- Train against several geolocators at once (OSV-5M and GeoViT) for blurs that transfer.
- Make the blur survive compression: apply JPEG and resizing to the blurred photo during training.
- More seeds, and optimized blur against GeoViT.
- Use the network in the demo: it is fast enough to protect a photo live.
